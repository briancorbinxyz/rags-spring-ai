# Schema-implied relations: deriving retrieval edges from a document-type grammar

Scope note: this covers (1) SEC form types as the canonical schema-implied relation family, (2) documented practice of graph/retrieval systems over filings, (3) schema/document-type grammars outside finance (DITA, S1000D, XBRL linkbases), (4) the requirements-traceability literature on recovered vs. authored links, (5) failure modes, (6) typed-edge selection for retrieval expansion.

Several primary documents I wanted (SEC `aqfs.pdf`, `form_13f_readme.pdf`, SEC release 33-11253, the Sundaram/Hayes/Dekhtyar RE Journal paper, the McMillan TEFSE'09 paper) are PDFs whose text I could not extract in this environment — `pdftotext`/`pdftoppm` are not installed and the fetch tool returned only compressed binary. Those are recorded as Gaps with the URL so the report writer can pull exact table definitions and figures if needed.

## Q1. SEC filings: what relations are defined by form type rather than stated in the document, and how EDGAR encodes them

### Takeaway
A large share of the useful relations in the EDGAR corpus are carried by the *form type plus its structured cover page*, not by prose or hyperlinks: who the filer is relative to an issuer (13D/13G), whether a report is a holdings report, a notice, or an amendment (13F-HR / 13F-NT / 13F-HR/A), and which issuers a filing names (the 13F information table's CUSIP column). EDGAR exposes these machine-readably through the form-type field, the accession number, the XML-derived data sets, and the XBRL APIs — but the SEC itself disclaims accuracy of the derived data sets, and its own Inspector General found nobody checks 13F filings for conformance.

### Cited Findings

**13F family — relations defined by the form type and its cover page**
- Form 13F is required of managers exercising investment discretion over **$100 million or more** in Section 13(f) securities; the reportable universe is not chosen by the filer but by the SEC's *Official List of Section 13(f) Securities*, which "primarily includes U.S. exchange-traded stocks," and mutual funds "are not included on the list and, therefore, should not be reported on Form 13F" — [SEC Division of Investment Management, Form 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/investment/13ffaq)
- The *manager → issuer* relation is a typed relation defined by the form, not by prose. "Investment discretion" exists where "the manager has the power to determine which securities are bought or sold for the account(s) under management"; it is reported as SOLE (you are the only manager and neither control nor are controlled by another reporting person), DEFINED ("If you control another entity (or are controlled by another entity), you should report shared-defined investment discretion"), or OTHER — [SEC Form 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/investment/13ffaq)
- The form type itself distinguishes three different *document-to-document* relations: **13F-HR** (all of your Section 13(f) securities are on your own form), a **13F Combination Report** (also filed as 13F-HR; some securities are on your form, the rest on another manager's form), and **13F-NT** (a Notice — all your securities are reported by another manager, and "when filing a Notice, you submit only a cover page"). Amendments are **13F-HR/A** and **13F-NT/A** — [SEC Form 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/investment/13ffaq)
- Consequence worth noting for retrieval: a 13F-NT is a pointer with no content. The relation "this manager's holdings are reported elsewhere" is expressible *only* from the form type and the other-managers list, never from the body text, because there is no body text.
- The information table columns are fixed by the instructions: issuer name "as it appears in the current official list of Section 13(f) Securities", CUSIP, title of class, value, shares/principal, investment discretion, other managers, and voting authority (sole/shared/none); positions in the same issuer and class are listed separately by sole vs. shared discretion, and every manager referenced in the "other managers" column must appear in the numbered list of other included managers — [example 13F-HR with instructions and sample tables](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001528086/000119312512238375/d355761d13fhr.txt)

**13D/13G — relations defined by which schedule you are eligible to file**
- The 2023 final rule (Modernization of Beneficial Ownership Reporting) shortened the initial Schedule 13D deadline from 10 days to **five business days** and requires Schedule 13D **amendments within two business days** — [SEC press release 2023-219](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-219); [SEC fact sheet, 33-11253](https://www.sec.gov/files/33-11253-fact-sheet.pdf)
- For Schedule 13G, qualified institutional investors and exempt investors file within **45 calendar days after the calendar quarter** in which the threshold is crossed; passive investors' initial deadline was cut from 10 days to five business days; 13G amendments are generally due 45 days after the *calendar quarter* in which a material change occurred (previously 45 days after the calendar year), with accelerated obligations for QIIs and passive investors when ownership crosses 10% or shifts by 5% — [SEC press release 2023-219](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-219); [SEC fact sheet](https://www.sec.gov/files/33-11253-fact-sheet.pdf)
- Compliance with the revised 13G deadlines began **Sept. 30, 2024**, and the **structured-data requirement for both schedules** had a compliance date of **Dec. 18, 2024** — [SEC press release 2023-219](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-219). This matters for a form-type-edge design: 13D/13G became machine-readable (structured) only from late 2024, so edge coverage is time-dependent.
- The SEC's staff interpretations for Regulation 13D-G are the authoritative source for who files which schedule and how amendments chain — [Exchange Act Sections 13(d)/13(g) and Regulation 13D-G Beneficial Ownership Reporting interpretations](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/corporation-finance-interpretations/exchange-act-sections-13d-13g-regulation-13d-g-beneficial-ownership-reporting)

**EDGAR's machine-readable encoding of these relations**
- The **submissions API** (`data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json`) keys an entity by 10-digit CIK and returns its filing history as a compact columnar array, with current name, former names, exchanges and tickers; longer histories are chunked into additional JSON files each labelled with the date range it covers — [SEC, EDGAR Application Programming Interfaces](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)
- The **XBRL APIs** cover facts from 10-Q, 10-K, 8-K, 20-F, 40-F, 6-K "and their variants", and include only facts using a *standard* (non-custom) taxonomy — us-gaap, ifrs-full, dei, srt — that apply to the whole filing entity. `companyconcept` returns every disclosure for one CIK and one concept with a separate array per unit; `companyfacts` returns all concepts for a company; `frames` returns one fact per reporting entity for a concept/unit/period (`CY####`, `CY####Q#`, `CY####Q#I`), using the most recently filed value that best fits the period — [SEC, EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)
- Relations the XBRL APIs therefore expose *without any authored link*: company→filings (submissions), company→concept→unit→fact (companyfacts), and **many companies joined through a shared concept/unit/period** (frames). **Custom taxonomy extensions are excluded** from the aggregated frames — [SEC, EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)
- The **Financial Statement Data Sets** contain numeric data extracted from XBRL-tagged filings, preserve "as filed" values in a flattened format "meant for comparing filings over time and across registrants", and add fields such as the registrant's SIC code. A **December 2024 reprocessing** restricted them to submissions and numeric data from the *primary financial statements as rendered by the Commission*, and added a `segments` field to the NUM file — [SEC, Financial Statement Data Sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/financial-statement-data-sets). Table-level definitions live in the separate readme PDF (see Gaps).
- The **Form 13F data sets** are "extracted from the XML-based portion of Form 13F submissions in EDGAR and presented without change from the 'as-filed' submissions" in a flattened format, covering July 2013 onward, released as quarterly ZIPs; a March 12, 2024 refresh realigned all historical sets to the 2023 format (EDGAR Release 22.4.1) and "fixed some missing information table records and certain filing manager address codes" — [SEC, Form 13F Data Sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets)
- The SEC's own disclaimer on that page: the data is derived from structured data filed by individual filers plus "Commission-generated filing identifiers", and "we cannot guarantee the accuracy of the data sets"; errors may also arise "during the process of extracting the data and compiling"; it "is not a substitute for the filings" — [SEC, Form 13F Data Sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets)
- **8-K item numbers are surfaced as structured metadata**: "EDGAR search results for reports on Form 8-K show the item numbers included in each report" — [SEC, Using EDGAR to research investments](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/using-edgar-research-investments). The 8-K item number (e.g. 2.02 results of operations, 5.02 director/officer changes) is therefore a form-type-level event classification available without reading the document.
- **Exhibits are where the form-type grammar leaks into authored links.** Exhibit indexes commonly *incorporate by reference* rather than attach, e.g. "Credit Agreement among the Partnership… (incorporated by reference to Exhibit 10.1 to Western's Current Report on Form 8-K, filed with the Commission on October 22, 2013)" — [Form 8-K/A Exhibit Index](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1339048/000133904815000004/form8-kaxexhibitindex.htm). And furnished items carry a disclaimer that breaks the apparent edge: "The information set forth in this Form 8-K and the attached exhibits shall not be deemed 'filed' for purposes of Section 18 of the Exchange Act, unless expressly stated" — [Bristow Group 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000073887/000007388710000037/form8k-05192010.htm)

### Inferences
- The strongest *schema-implied* edges in EDGAR are the ones with a cardinality the form fixes: 13F-HR → (CUSIP, issuer) is many-to-many but each row is an assertion of a typed relation; 13F-NT → other manager is 1-to-few; `/A` → base filing is 1-to-1 within a CIK + period. These are cheap, high-confidence edges.
- The weakest are the ones the form merely *permits*: an exhibit index may point anywhere, and a "furnished" 8-K exhibit is explicitly not incorporated. An edge derived from "8-K has exhibits" fires on nearly every 8-K and so carries almost no information — this is the fan-out case in miniature.
- Because 13D/13G only became structured from Dec 2024, a form-type-edge pipeline over a historical corpus will have to parse unstructured cover pages for the earlier period, which reintroduces exactly the extraction error the schema was supposed to remove.

### Gaps
- Exact table and field definitions for the Financial Statement Data Sets (SUB/TAG/NUM/PRE, the `adsh` accession-number key, the `form` field) — documented only in [https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfs.pdf](https://www.sec.gov/files/aqfs.pdf), which I could not extract text from. Same for the 13F data set tables (COVERPAGE, SUMMARYPAGE, OTHERMANAGER, INFOTABLE, SIGNATURE) in [https://www.sec.gov/files/form_13f_readme.pdf](https://www.sec.gov/files/form_13f_readme.pdf).
- I could not retrieve a primary SEC statement of the accession-number *format* (the `0001234567-YY-NNNNNN` grammar) or an official statement that it encodes filer-agent + year + sequence. Do not assert that structure without a citation.
- The full text of release [33-11253](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11253.pdf) (eligibility criteria distinguishing 13D from 13G filers — activist vs. qualified institutional vs. passive — and the Rule 13d-2 amendment trigger wording) is unextracted; eCFR blocked the fetch (302 to an unblock page).
- I found no official SEC document that *names* the inter-filing relations as a taxonomy (i.e. nothing like a published EDGAR relation schema). The relations are implied by each form's instructions, separately.

## Q2. Documented practice of building retrieval/graph systems over filings using form-type-implied relations

### Takeaway
Practice exists and is converging on a document → section/chunk → entity graph with form type and filing date as filters or edge properties — but I found **no evaluated system whose edges are derived from the SEC form-type grammar**, and no benchmark isolating the contribution of form-type edges. The available artifacts are project READMEs, a vendor blog, and recent preprints; this is a clear case of a widespread practice that is unevaluated.

### Cited Findings
- **sec-graph** ingests 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K filings, lets the user filter by form type and year range, and answers e.g. acquisition-history questions by filtering `ACQUIRED` relationship edges by `filed_date`; it combines Neo4j traversal with Qdrant vector search, and clusters `EXPOSED_TO_RISK` edges for risk questions — [Jumade/sec-graph](https://github.com/Jumade/sec-graph). Note the edges here are **LLM-extracted from text**, not derived from form type; form type is a *filter*, not an edge.
- **SEC-GraphRAG** is a small pilot: 16 chunks, 63 entities, 94 evidence edges from 47 listed subsidiary rows across four subsidiary exhibits. The author explicitly records the coverage limit: no DEF 14A proxy filings, so board-member connections and full company coverage are out of scope — [S-15-77/SEC-GraphRAG](https://github.com/S-15-77/SEC-GraphRAG). This is the Exhibit 21 (subsidiaries) case — a form-type-defined exhibit yielding typed `SUBSIDIARY_OF` edges.
- **AWS "Agentic GraphRAG for Capital Markets"** built roughly **600,000 subject-relation-object triplets** from 2024 10-Ks of **101 S&P 100 companies**, using **24 entity types and 27 relationship types**, retaining the source text chunk per triplet. The schema is Document → Chunk → Entity/Event (documents contain chunks, chunks mention entities and events, entities act on events). Retrieval mixes graph traversal with semantic search — [AWS Industries blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/agentic-graphrag-for-capital-markets/). No retrieval accuracy metrics are reported in the post.
- **HC-RAG** is the closest published work to a *structure-derived* edge design over filings: edge types follow the document's own structure — **containment** edges (document→section, section→evidence unit), **sequential** edges preserving section/unit order, **cross** edges joining related companies, fiscal years, sectors or financial metrics, and **modal** edges linking narrative text to table evidence; retrieval follows document → section → evidence-unit paths, which it argues improves traceability of results to source — [HC-RAG preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12335)
- An **agentic graph RAG for commercial registry analysis** preprint exists in the same space (auditability over registry filings) — [arXiv 2605.18770](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18770)
- Patent filings in this area describe schema-based retrieval from knowledge graphs and understanding of financial documents, i.e. commercial interest in exactly this mechanism — [US 11,907,182 "Schema-based data retrieval from knowledge graphs"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11907182); [US 12,271,914 "Method and system for understanding financial documents"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12271914)

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern across all of these is that **form type is used as a pre-retrieval filter or an edge attribute, not as the source of the edge**. The one system that derives edges from structure (HC-RAG) derives them from *intra-document* structure (sections, tables), not from the form-type taxonomy across documents.
- The Exhibit 21 pilot is the existence proof that a form-type-defined exhibit can be turned into typed edges mechanically, and the 94-edges-from-47-rows ratio suggests roughly 2 edges per schema row — cheap and high precision, but with the coverage hole the author names.
- Nobody has published an ablation of form-type-implied edges. If this is the design you want to justify, you will likely have to produce the measurement yourself; the literature will not supply it.

### Gaps
- No measured retrieval accuracy (nDCG, recall@k, precision@k) attributable to form-type edges in any source I found.
- The 2026 preprints (HC-RAG, the registry paper) are recent and I verified only the search-surfaced descriptions, not the full papers; the report writer should confirm numbers before citing any.
- I found no account of what *broke* in a production filings graph, beyond the coverage caveat in the SEC-GraphRAG README. No post-mortems.

## Q3. Evidence on deriving edges from a schema or document-type system generally (DITA, S1000D, DocBook, XBRL)

### Takeaway
The document-type standards are explicit that schema-level structures (relationship tables, publication modules, linkbases) *are* the link mechanism, and DITA in particular is explicit that the standard defines **only explicit links** — implicit links are left to processors, i.e. unspecified and unevaluated. These standards give you a well-defined grammar, but no measured retrieval evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **DITA formally defines only explicit links**, "although processors may implement implicit links" — [OASIS DITA 1.2 architectural spec, DITA linking](https://docs.oasis-open.org/dita/v1.2/os/spec/archSpec/ditalinking.html)
- A `<reltable>` "defines relationships that processors can express as topic-to-topic links in generated output" — relations declared once in the map, not authored in the topics — [DITA: How relationship tables establish linking relationships](https://dita-lang.org/dita/archspec/base/establishing-links-with-reltable)
- Row semantics: "Topic references in different cells of the same row are related. By default, topic references that are in the same cell do not establish linking relationships to each other." Links are reciprocal by default; `linking="targetonly"` on a cell means "the row establishes links from other cells to that cell, but does not establish links from that cell out to other topics in the row" — [DITA reltable spec](https://dita-lang.org/dita/archspec/base/establishing-links-with-reltable)
- "By default, relationship-table content is not rendered as part of navigation structures such as a table of contents" — the derived edges are a retrieval/see-also structure distinct from the navigation hierarchy — [DITA reltable spec](https://dita-lang.org/dita/archspec/base/establishing-links-with-reltable)
- Scope composition: "Within a map tree, the effective set of relationship tables is the union of all the relationship tables in all the maps in the map tree" — edges compose across maps, which is where unexpected fan-out can arise — [DITA reltable spec](https://dita-lang.org/dita/archspec/base/establishing-links-with-reltable)
- Core map elements and `<topicref>` hierarchy are defined in [OASIS DITA 1.3 language reference, basic map elements](https://docs.oasis-open.org/dita/dita/v1.3/os/part1-base/langRef/containers/basic-map-elements.html) and [DITA 1.1 map elements](https://docs.oasis-open.org/dita/v1.1/OS/langspec/common/map2.html)
- **S1000D**: the Data Module Code is a compound identifier (model identification code allocated per project by NAMSA, system difference code identifying alternative systems sharing identical SNS codes, system code, etc.), stored inside the data module *and* forming part of the file name. Each data module splits into an identification-and-status section (CSDB management, applicability, QA, retrieval) and a content section — [The Data Module Code, Markup UK 2018](https://markupuk.org/2018/webhelp/ar02s03s01s01.html); [Data Modules](https://markupuk.org/2018/webhelp/ar02s01s03s01.html)
- The same source notes the interpretive limit of code-derived semantics: codes such as model and system "make a lot of sense for aircraft engine components but are harder to interpret for general procedural documents" — [Markup UK, The Data Module Code](https://markupuk.org/2018/webhelp/ar02s03s01s01.html)
- In S1000D the explicit linkage lives in control files — the Common Source Database stores and relates data modules, a Publication Module "defines the structure, sequence, and inclusion of Data Modules in a deliverable publication", and a Data Module Requirement List records which modules are required — [S1000D Getting Started](https://s1000d.org/?page_id=2); [NSWCCD S1000D IETM Acquisition Guide](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/NSWC_Carderock/NSWCCD-20-TR-2011-06%20S1000D%20IETM%20Acq%20Guide%202011Oct.pdf)
- **XBRL (as used by SEC)**: the SEC maintains "current taxonomies supported for the Commission's Interactive Data programs", grouped for operating companies, investment companies, SROs, security-based swap entities and NRSROs — [SEC, Standard Taxonomies](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/standard-taxonomies). The practical schema-implied relation for retrieval is the concept: `frames` joins many companies on one concept/unit/period with no authored link at all — [SEC, EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)

### Inferences
- DITA's reltable is the cleanest conceptual precedent for the design under evaluation: relations are declared at the type/collection level and materialised as links at build time. Its two safety valves are worth copying — directionality control (`targetonly`) and keeping derived links *out* of the primary navigation so they cannot drown it.
- S1000D is the counter-lesson: identifiers that encode a domain hierarchy give you cheap structural edges inside the domain they were designed for, and become arbitrary outside it. A code-derived edge is only as meaningful as the issuing authority's taxonomy.
- Across all three standards, the schema tells you an edge *may* exist; none of them quantifies how useful it is for retrieval. That evidence does not exist in the standards literature.

### Gaps
- I did not find any source describing the rules implicit-link processors actually use in DITA, so "implicit DITA links" cannot be characterised beyond "unspecified".
- No DocBook primary source retrieved. DocBook's `olink`/`xref` mechanism was not verified; do not assert claims about it.
- No source found on S1000D BREX (Business Rules Exchange) as a conformance-checking mechanism, which would have been the natural evidence for type-conformance enforcement.
- No measured study of retrieval over a DITA/S1000D corpus using reltable- or DMC-derived edges. I believe none exists in the open literature, but I cannot prove absence.

## Q4. Requirements traceability: established methods, reported recall/precision, and schema-implied vs. authored links

### Takeaway
Two decades of IR-based trace recovery converge on a hard precision ceiling: high recall is achievable but precision at useful recall is poor, results are strongly dataset-dependent, and no IR model consistently wins. The literature's own answer to the precision problem is to **add structural information** to textual similarity — which is the argument for schema-derived edges, though the direct evidence is described by its authors as preliminary.

### Cited Findings
- **Standards frame traceability as an obligation, not a measurement.** "Bidirectional traceability" is defined as an "association among two or more logical entities that are discernible in either direction" (ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765:2010, cited by NASA's handbook) — [NASA SWEHB SWE-059](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/7150/pages/16449675/SWE-059+-+Bidirectional+Traceability+Between+Software+Requirements+and+Software+Design)
- NASA's guidance names the two failure directions the trace is meant to catch: it "can point out software design elements that are not fulfilled in the code, as well as source code that does not have a parent design element", and says "ideally, the trace does not identify any elements that have no source", with orphans reviewed to decide whether they are needed — [NASA SWEHB SWE-059](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/7150/pages/16449675/SWE-059+-+Bidirectional+Traceability+Between+Software+Requirements+and+Software+Design)
- NASA also notes the staleness problem directly: for small projects "the project must keep traces current, since spreadsheets and documents do not update automatically when requirements or design change" — [NASA SWEHB SWE-059](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/7150/pages/16449675/SWE-059+-+Bidirectional+Traceability+Between+Software+Requirements+and+Software+Design)
- **DO-178C** requires documented bidirectional traces between certification artifacts; a low-level requirement traces up to the high-level requirement it satisfies and down to the code and tests that verify it — [DO-178C summary](https://quality.arc42.org/standards/do-178c). The revision was intended "to eliminate practices such as embedding traceability information in the operational code, or test code" — [Military & Aerospace Electronics, DO-178C core changes](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/commercial-aerospace/article/14224954/do-178c-core-changes)
- DO-178C's hard consequence for an orphan: "Elements with no upstream requirement are classified as extraneous code and constitute a certification finding regardless of whether they function correctly" — [Model-driven framework for requirement traceability](https://ftp.saiconference.com/Downloads/Volume12No2/Paper_1-Model_driven_Framework_for_Requirement_Traceability.pdf). Full bidirectional traceability across four levels of specification hierarchy is required at DAL A; low-level requirement objectives do not apply at Level D — [same source](https://ftp.saiconference.com/Downloads/Volume12No2/Paper_1-Model_driven_Framework_for_Requirement_Traceability.pdf)
- **RETRO (Hayes, Dekhtyar, Sundaram)** is the canonical IR trace-recovery tool. In a study with student subjects the tool produced **higher recall than manual tracing but lower precision**, and was faster — [Evaluation of Traceability Recovery in Context: A Taxonomy for IR Tools](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230560206_Evaluation_of_Traceability_Recovery_in_Context_A_Taxonomy_for_Information_Retrieval_Tools); [RETRO paper](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/REquirements-TRacing-On-target-(RETRO):-improving-Hayes-Dekhtyar/8b88ccda5f420412c38bfcd5026824b82238846e)
- Hayes and Dekhtyar proposed **"Acceptable", "Good" and "Excellent" intervals** for recall and precision based on practical experience — i.e. the field conceded that raw P/R needed interpretive bands — [Taxonomy for IR Tools](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230560206_Evaluation_of_Traceability_Recovery_in_Context_A_Taxonomy_for_Information_Retrieval_Tools)
- Sundaram, Hayes, Dekhtyar and Holbrook argue precision and recall alone do not capture analyst effort, and add secondary measures; with high recall, **the size of the candidate matrix estimates the human effort required** — [Assessing traceability of software engineering artifacts, Requirements Engineering Journal 2007](http://selab.netlab.uky.edu/homepage/publications/rej_2007_sundaram_hayes_dekhtyar_holbrook.pdf)
- **Borg, Runeson and Ardö's systematic mapping** ("Recovering from a decade of progress", *Empirical Software Engineering* 19 (2014) 1565–1616) classified **79 publications**, a majority applying algebraic IR models, and found **no empirical evidence that any IR model consistently outperforms another**; they report "a strong dependency between the P-R values and the input datasets", that mapping P-R values onto the possible output space "highlights the difficulty of recovering accurate trace links using naïve cut-off strategies", and that most earlier studies do not go beyond reporting P/R "from evaluations using datasets containing less than 500 artifacts" — [Markus Borg's page for the EMSE'15 study](http://mrksbrg.com/emse15_borg/); [TraceRepo supplementary material](https://sites.google.com/site/tracerepo)
- **The precision ceiling, stated as motivation in the literature:** text-only methods "tend to have low precision at reasonable recall, because they rely upon the syntactical features of words", so they struggle with artifacts that are not syntactically related — [Combining Textual and Structural Analysis of Software Artifacts, TEFSE 2009](https://sdf.org/~cmc/papers/mcmillan_tefse09.pdf)
- **The schema/structure answer:** the same TEFSE'09 paper "combines textual with structural information, conjecturing that related requirements share related source code elements", and reports "a preliminary case study indicates that the combined approach improves precision and recall relative to stand-alone textual methods" — [McMillan et al., TEFSE 2009](https://sdf.org/~cmc/papers/mcmillan_tefse09.pdf)
- A later literature review confirms the line of work: research "investigated structural dependencies within source code, such as call and inheritance relationships, to further improve results" — [A Literature Review of Automatic Traceability Links Recovery, ICPC 2020](https://yuleisui.github.io/publications/icpc20.pdf)
- A 2025 study on formal-specification-to-code links uses multi-dimensional attributes (names, structures, relationships) and reports better average precision, recall and F1 than text-based similarity techniques including LSI, VSM and embedding-based methods — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0164121225001074)
- Work on *relations among target artifacts* is directly analogous to form-type edges: "Analyzing close relations between target artifacts for improving IR-based requirement traceability recovery" — [FITEE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1631/FITEE.2000126)
- Narrowing the textual signal also helps precision: restricting retrieval to **nouns** reduced false positives, "especially at 100% recall" — [On the role of the nouns in IR-based traceability recovery](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224516895_On_the_role_of_the_nouns_in_IR-based_traceability_recovery)
- Other reported figures, flagged as unverified against originals: one earlier study reached **85% recall at 40% precision** on a small dataset using tf-idf, per the Sundaram et al. RE Journal paper; a later *search-based* (not IR) approach reported **86% precision and 83% recall** averaged over three projects — [Sundaram et al.](http://selab.netlab.uky.edu/homepage/publications/rej_2007_sundaram_hayes_dekhtyar_holbrook.pdf); [Search-Based Requirements Traceability Recovery](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319198686_Search-Based_Requirements_Traceability_Recovery)

### Inferences
- The traceability field's verdict maps almost exactly onto a precision-sensitive top-4 retrieval problem: IR similarity gets you recall cheaply and precision expensively, and the documented way to buy precision is to add structure that is *not* textual. A form-type grammar is exactly such a structure.
- Borg et al.'s "strong dependency between P-R values and the input datasets" is the warning against transferring any published number: a form-type-edge design has to be measured on the target corpus. Their observation about naïve cut-off strategies is the direct analogue of "an edge that always fires" — the problem is choosing *which* candidate links to keep, not generating them.
- The standards (DO-178C, ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148/24765) require bidirectional traceability but say nothing about recovering it automatically; they are evidence that schema-level trace *obligations* are mature while automated recovery remains a measurement problem.

## Q5. Documented failure modes of schema-derived edges

### Takeaway
All four named failure modes have documented support, though from uneven sources: fan-out/hub bias is measured (weakly) in graph RAG; stale and orphan artifacts are a first-class concern in traceability standards; type-conformance violations in a real corpus are documented at EDGAR by the SEC's own Inspector General; and the precision cost of an always-firing edge is the traceability field's central finding, restated as the cut-off problem.

### Cited Findings

**Fan-out / hub bias**
- "A fundamental limitation of static graph retrieval is Hub Bias (or degree centrality bias)." A personalized-PageRank approach (CatRAG) shifts mass away from high-degree nodes versus HippoRAG 2, but only modestly: the share of probability mass on the top 1% "super hubs" drops from **45.7% to 42.5%** — [Breaking the Static Graph: Context-Aware Traversal for Robust RAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.01965)
- NVIDIA's GraphRAG tutorial reports that Cypher retrieval "returns large subgraphs with high recall but low precision", and lists "high average node degree causing neighborhood explosion" as a problem in its biomedical QA example — [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/boosting-qa-accuracy-with-graphrag-using-pyg-and-graph-databases/)
- The same effect at the embedding level: "hub embeddings dominate neighbor lists, causing systematic retrieval bias" — [RAGRouter-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00296)
- Cost, not just precision: super nodes "cause cascading latency during multi-hop traversals because every connected edge must be evaluated"; the recommendation is to partition high-degree nodes (vendor source, treat thresholds with caution) — [FalkorDB blog](https://www.falkordb.com/blog/graph-database-anti-patterns-ai-performance/)
- Background definition of hubs in network science — [Hub (network science)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hub_(network_science))

**Type-conformance violations in a real corpus (the strongest finding in this section)**
- The SEC Office of Inspector General's *Review of the SEC's Section 13(f) Reporting Requirements* found that **no SEC division or office monitors Form 13F filings for accuracy and completeness**, and that EDGAR has no built-in checks for obvious errors; "as a result, many Forms 13F are filed with errors or problems, which may not be detected or corrected in a timely manner" — [SEC OIG report 480](https://www.sec.gov/files/480.pdf)
- Anderson and Brockman, *Form 13F (Mis)Filings*: using hand-collected bank holding company filings, reported holdings do not always appear on the SEC's Official List, market prices of Official List securities are often inaccurate, and **amended 13F reports are sometimes less accurate than the originals**; they conclude that reliance on 13F filings is unwarranted for private investors and capital markets researchers — [SSRN 2809128](https://www.ssrn.com/abstract=2809128); [Harvard Law corpgov summary](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2016/08/08/form-13f-mis-filings/)
- The SEC's own data-set disclaimer, repeated here because it is the Commission conceding the point: "we cannot guarantee the accuracy of the data sets" — [SEC, Form 13F Data Sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets)
- Researchers routinely drop data for conformance reasons: an arXiv statistical study of 13F-HR excluded earlier quarters "due to low quality of reporting" — [arXiv 2209.08825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.08825)
- Vendor-reported recurring defect taxonomy (unverified, 2026): values reported in thousands rather than dollars, inconsistent issuer names, CUSIPs that break on corporate actions, invalid identifiers — [FocusAlpha](https://focusalpha.ai/blog/13f-filing-deadline)
- Practitioner warning in the same direction — [Alpha Architect, "Beware of institutional ownership data from 13F filings!"](https://alphaarchitect.com/beware-of-institutional-ownership-data-from-13f-filings/)

**Stale or absent artifacts / orphans**
- Spreadsheet-based traces "do not update automatically when requirements or design change", so the project must actively keep them current — [NASA SWEHB SWE-059](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/7150/pages/16449675/SWE-059+-+Bidirectional+Traceability+Between+Software+Requirements+and+Software+Design)
- Orphan handling is normative: elements with no source should be reviewed ([NASA SWEHB](https://swehb.nasa.gov/spaces/7150/pages/16449675/SWE-059+-+Bidirectional+Traceability+Between+Software+Requirements+and+Software+Design)); and in DO-178C an element with no upstream requirement is "extraneous code" and a certification finding "regardless of whether they function correctly" — [model-driven traceability framework paper](https://ftp.saiconference.com/Downloads/Volume12No2/Paper_1-Model_driven_Framework_for_Requirement_Traceability.pdf)
- In EDGAR terms, the analogue is a 13F-NT whose referenced manager's filing is missing or late, or a `/A` amendment whose base filing is absent from the local corpus; the SEC's own fix-up of "missing information table records" in the March 2024 refresh is evidence that absent records occur even in the official data sets — [SEC, Form 13F Data Sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets)

**The precision cost of an edge that always fires**
- Borg et al.: naïve cut-off strategies make accurate trace link recovery difficult, and P-R values depend strongly on the dataset — [EMSE'15 study page](http://mrksbrg.com/emse15_borg/)
- Analyst effort scales with the candidate set: with high recall, the size of the candidate matrix estimates human effort — [Sundaram et al.](http://selab.netlab.uky.edu/homepage/publications/rej_2007_sundaram_hayes_dekhtyar_holbrook.pdf)
- PAGE-RAG's design stance is the cleanest statement of the mitigation: "a graph edge is treated as a proposal for support, not as support by default" — [PAGE-RAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29753)

### Inferences
- The four failure modes are not independent: a type that relates to everything (every 10-K has exhibits; every large manager holds Apple) produces a hub, the hub produces fan-out, and the fan-out is what converts a high-recall edge into a precision loss at top-4.
- The EDGAR conformance evidence is the most important finding for the engineering decision. A form-type grammar is only a *contract*, and the SEC OIG says the contract is unpoliced for 13F. So a form-type edge should be treated as a *claim to verify*, not a fact — consistent with PAGE-RAG's "proposal for support".
- An honest design rule follows from the hub-bias numbers: because the published mitigations (PPR reweighting) move hub mass only a few percentage points, degree capping or type-level suppression at index time is more likely to help than traversal-time reweighting.

### Gaps
- **I found no study that measures precision loss as a function of schema-edge fan-out.** The hub-bias evidence is about LLM-extracted entity graphs, not schema-derived edges, and the only quantity I can cite (45.7% → 42.5% super-hub mass) is a proxy, not a precision curve. This is a genuine evidence gap.
- No study quantifying the rate of form-type conformance violations in EDGAR (e.g. what fraction of `/A` filings lack a resolvable base filing). The OIG report establishes that errors exist and are unmonitored, not how many.
- The OIG report predates later 13F rule changes, so its findings may be dated.

## Q6. Typed edge selection for retrieval expansion — choosing which relation to follow given the question

### Takeaway
Yes, this is an active and explicitly typed line of work: LLM-driven relation selection (BYOKG-RAG), type- and strength-gated expansion (ConMem), and typed query-local graphs where edges are treated as proposals (PAGE-RAG). The mechanism is published; per-relation-type retrieval metrics are not, in what I could reach.

### Cited Findings
- **BYOKG-RAG** performs relation selection as an explicit retrieval step: an LLM prompt "select[s] the most appropriate relations based on their relevance to a given question" from a candidate list during agentic graph traversal, asking for a *frugal but complete* selection and requiring at least one relation — [BYOKG-RAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04127)
- **Relation-based query expansion** is described in the GraphRAG survey: relational extraction maps a relation phrase in the query to matching graph edges, which guides traversal direction, and query expansion in GraphRAG augments LLM expansion with structured relations — [Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Graphs (GraphRAG) survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00309v2)
- **ConMem** gives each edge a relation type and a strength weight, and traverses "only admissible edges with sufficient strength" — i.e. the edge type itself gates expansion — [ConMem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08702)
- **PAGE-RAG** builds a query-local graph with five edge types including document–sentence containment, sentence–entity mention, and entity–entity relation links, under a fixed retrieval budget, and treats each edge as "a proposal for support, not as support by default" — [PAGE-RAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29753)
- Practitioner guidance (not benchmarked): keep the relation schema small, prefer general relation types over many specific ones, and filter by type at query time — [Medium, GraphRAG structured retrieval](https://medium.com/@akdemir_bahadir/graphrag-structured-retrieval-and-reasoning-over-knowledge-graphs-d819490bbf4a); [From RAG to Agentic Retrieval](https://medium.com/@fahey_james/from-rag-graph-rag-agentic-retrieval-designing-retrieval-that-thinks-a458115a8a13); [LogRocket, Graph RAG explained](https://blog.logrocket.com/graph-rag-relationship-retrieval/)
- Overviews of graph-RAG retrieval patterns for context — [Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Emergent Mind)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/graph-retrieval-augmented-generation-rag); [Hub-Based Graph Algorithms](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/hub-based-graph-algorithms)

### Inferences
- Typed-edge selection is the natural answer to the fan-out problem a schema-derived graph creates: a document-type grammar gives a *small, closed* relation vocabulary (amends, reports-holdings-of, is-notice-for, is-exhibit-to, same-concept-as), which is exactly the "small schema with general types" that both BYOKG-RAG's prompt-based selection and the practitioner guidance want. A form-type grammar is a better fit for typed selection than an LLM-extracted open relation set, because the candidate relation list is enumerable and stable.
- For a top-4-scored system, the PAGE-RAG framing (edge as proposal, fixed budget) plus ConMem-style type gating is the design most defensible from the published evidence: expand only on relation types the question licenses, and let the expanded candidates compete rather than be promoted.

### Gaps
- No ablation found that reports retrieval metrics per relation type, or the gain from relation selection versus following all edges. BYOKG-RAG's and ConMem's numbers were not verified from full text.
- Several of the strongest-looking sources here are 2026 preprints surfaced via search (arXiv 2602.01965, 2602.00296, 2606.08702, 2608.29753, 2608.12335, 2605.18770). I verified only their search-surfaced descriptions and quoted fragments, not the full papers. The report writer should confirm each before citing a number from it.
- I found no IR/RAG paper that frames the problem as "schema-implied edges" in the terms this research question uses; the vocabulary in the literature is "structural edges", "relation types", or "containment edges".
