# Don't build the graph stage; fix the parser

**Verdict: no. Do not build a graph index or a recursive link traversal.** The golden set contains **zero questions that need two hops**. It contains 11 one-hop chained questions, every one of them in the `relation` stratum, and the best current configuration already answers most of them. The arithmetic ceiling for *any* new mechanism is **16 of 105 questions**, and once you subtract the cases the saved runs already explain by reranking, depth or policy, the honest remainder is **5 to 7 questions** — all of them in the held-out and time sets, none in the published main line, which is saturated at **Hit@4 47/47**. Meanwhile the corpus is the opposite of a GraphRAG candidate: **72.9% of its concrete, instance-level relations are already written down as links** ([declared_vs_latent_edges.md](/Users/briancorbin/Code/rags/rags-spring-ai/research_notes/Multi%20hop%20questions%20and%20graph%20retrieval/declared_vs_latent_edges.md)), and the one latent family with real mass is the one whose precision collapses exactly where its recall lives. GraphRAG's headline claim — that its value is relating documents whose links were never called out — **has never been isolated in any published ablation**, and every edge-provenance ablation that does exist finds declared structural edges carrying the larger share ([multihop_external_evidence.md](/Users/briancorbin/Code/rags/rags-spring-ai/research_notes/Multi%20hop%20questions%20and%20graph%20retrieval/multihop_external_evidence.md)). The work that pays is not a new relation type. It is the roughly **2,000 declared relation values our own parser currently throws away**, the **1,218 edges that point at a page instead of a unit**, and the fact that the expansion trigger can fire on **4 of 105 questions**.

All four notes agree on the direction. Where they carry a caveat — stale, pre-audit, snippet-level, unverified, laptop-tier — it is carried here too.

## The question set has 11 one-hop chains and not a single bridge

Of the 105 questions, **74 are single-passage, 13 are multi-evidence parallel, 11 are multi-hop at exactly one hop, 0 are bridges, and 7 are unanswerable** ([hop_structure.md](/Users/briancorbin/Code/rags/rags-spring-ai/research_notes/Multi%20hop%20questions%20and%20graph%20retrieval/hop_structure.md)). The 11 chained questions are **G-030, G-031, G-032, G-033, G-036** in the main set and **H-001, H-003, H-004, H-006, H-007, H-008** in the held-out set. Every one sits in the `relation` stratum except H-008, which is a `history` question whose structure is a supersession link. The time set contributes **zero** chained questions. The routing set contributes zero, and that zero is deliberate: R-005, R-007, R-009 and R-014 each record a second, topically relevant document and rule it out of the gold on purpose.

The negative result is the important one. **No question in any of the four golden files requires an A → B → C read.** The only questions with three or more expected addresses are G-076 (3, all in one spec) and G-055 (4, all in one file), and both are parallel. The single near-miss is **G-033**, whose pointer in `specs/023-explore-graduation/design.html#D-006` is the placeholder `TBD-explore-restore`, with the mapping to spec 024 recorded in `specs/024-explore-restore/tasks.html` — a document that is not in `expected`. It collapses to one hop in practice because the slug `explore-restore` string-matches the directory name. The golden note already flags it: *"Brian should check he accepts that hop."* That is your call and this report does not make it. The same applies to **H-013**, classed multi-evidence because the question names both commands' documents, with the reasoning recorded rather than hidden.

The structural reason there are no bridges matters more than the count. spectastic's typed links — `validates`, `defer-to`, dependency rows, supersession notes — are all one-step relations between an element and another element or spec. Nothing in the document grammar composes two relations. A 2-hop question would have had to be invented by chaining two different link types.

The pointer behind each chain is where the case for link-following actually breaks. Six of the 11 chains have a typed attribute at hop 1, one has a plain hyperlink, four have a bare identifier in prose with no link at all. But the sharper split is granularity: **only 3 of 11 pointers name the destination element** (G-030 and H-001 via `validates`, H-008 via a real `<a href=…#FR-007>`). The other eight name only a *spec*, so completing the chain needs a within-spec content search after the hop. A graph index over typed links would fully solve 3 of 11 and half-solve 8. The four prose cases — `013-core-propose` inside a thrown-error string, `011's` as a possessive, `071 US1 → US2` in an ASCII table, `TBD-explore-restore` as a placeholder — are the category no markup-derived graph can capture at all.

Two further structures are worth separating from "multi-hop". G-030 and H-001 both have **hop 1 unscored**: a retriever that lands SC-002, the only thing the question names, scores nothing, because `expected` holds the far end only. Those are the two questions where Hit@k most understates what the system found. And the time set has a genuinely different shape: **11 of its 24 questions are latent multi-hop, scored single** (16 across the whole universe). G-067 through G-071 all name change proposals by slug in their reference answers, and those slugs come only from a `#changelog` the question never names and the metric never scores. The `#changelog` → `changes/**/proposal.html` edge is a real `<a href>`; it is scored in exactly 2 of 24 time questions (G-051, G-052) and required-but-unscored in at least 5. Whether a retriever *could* answer G-067–G-071 from the single expected address alone is a relevance judgment, explicitly left to you.

## The declared graph is correct, tiny, and 30% unusable

The parser emits eight edge kinds — **12,283 raw rows, 11,258 distinct triples** over 10,540 unit keys and 627 pages — plus supersession, which is kept in two separate dicts and is not an edge at all ([declared_vs_latent_edges.md](/Users/briancorbin/Code/rags/rags-spring-ai/research_notes/Multi%20hop%20questions%20and%20graph%20retrieval/declared_vs_latent_edges.md)). Every figure below is over unit keys, independently recomputed and in exact agreement with `declared_meta.md`'s inventory.

| fact | figure |
|---|---|
| unit keys with no edge of any kind | **4,372 (41.5%)** |
| unit keys with no unit→unit neighbour | **5,352 (50.8%)** |
| `typed_neighbours` non-empty | **1,571 (14.9%)**, mean 1.74, median 1.0, max 26 |
| `EXPAND_MAX = 5` actually binds | 84 of 1,571 (**5.3%**) |
| rows with a page end (unusable anchored) | **3,621 (29.5%)**, of which u→p 1,218 |
| `typed:defer-to` that is u→p | **166 of 166 (100%)** |
| `href:same-spec` page coverage | **624 of 627 pages (99.5%)** |

Two things in that table decide the question. First, **the typed graph is high-precision and almost absent**: coverage runs 0.8% to 9.0% per kind with mean fan-out 1.10 to 1.98. The constraint on expansion is the absence of edges, not the cap. Second, **`href:same-spec` collapsed to page granularity carries almost no information** — it says "this page is in the same spec directory as that one", true of 99.5% of pages. That is the mechanical explanation for config **EpiB scoring 36/47 against E5-1's 44/47**, winning G-029 and displacing nine hits ([edge_value_measurement.md](/Users/briancorbin/Code/rags/rags-spring-ai/research_notes/Schema%20implied%20relations%20in%20spectastic/edge_value_measurement.md) reports the losses; the 99.5% is why).

Now the latent side. **5,074 of 6,963 countable instance-level relations (72.9%) are already declared**. Cross-artifact prose mentions are 72.3% declared, sibling artifact page pairs 76.3% (and the five schema-mandated sibling pairs are **100%**), changelog-by-date 76.3%. The same-id hypothesis is **dead and actively harmful**: 86 ID-shaped same-directory pairs, **0 declared and 0 correct** — all `tasks.html#T-nnn` versus `triage-log.html#T-nnn` namespace collisions between unrelated items — plus 573 vacuous section-anchor pairs. That reproduces `anchor-dir`'s measured reach of **0/8** exactly. The only family with real latent mass, cross-spec concept co-reference, degrades monotonically into redundancy: at ≥20 shared distinctive tokens **74.7% of surviving pairs are already linked**, and at ≥1 token it admits 57% of all possible spec pairs, so it is barely a filter.

None of the countable latent families needs an LLM. Prose identifier mentions are a regex plus `resolve_id`, which `corpus._links` already contains. Sibling relations are one `rsplit`, and `siblings()` already does it. Changelog dates are an ISO-date regex against a directory listing. **This is an argument from derivability, not a head-to-head measurement** — no LLM extractor was run and no saved extraction artefact exists, and the note says so plainly.

What *is* being left on the table is declared, not latent. Taking `declared_meta.md`'s counts as given and not re-derived: **`target` 770 declared against 423 extracted (~45% loss), `defer-to` 674 declared against 166 extracted (~75% loss)**, and four relation attributes extracted not at all — `spec-decision grounding` (469), `spec-parent specid` (22, the only explicit spec-to-spec hierarchy in the archive), `spec-risk status`/`by` (360/242), `spec-triage layer` (~168). That is roughly **2,000 declared relation values currently discarded**, against a latent population of 1,668 prose mentions and 184 sibling pairs.

Then the gate. Expansion runs only `if cfg.expand and pinned_keys`, seeded from `pinned_keys[0]` alone. Across all 105 questions **12 (11.4%) pin anything, and only 4 (3.8%) have a first-pinned anchor with any typed neighbour or typed page**. The routing set pins 0 of 15; the time set pins 1 of 24 and **0** can expand. Even a perfect LLM-extracted graph inserted behind this trigger changes at most 4 of 105 answers. This figure comes from a faithful re-implementation of `IdIndex.lookup`, not from executing the prototype's own code; it cross-checks against the 8/8 `identifier`-stratum pin rate and the 1-of-13 held-out rate the earlier note implies.

Finally, bitemporality. **The time corpus has no edges at all** — `timecorpus-c64d41c-v1.json` has three keys and `timecorpus.py` contains no edge structure. So no edge carries a validity interval, and the consequence is large: of the 7,956 distinct unit→unit snapshot edges, only **27.6% had both endpoints in force at position 300 of 721**, and **20.9% at position 200**. At p=300, **386 edges have their near end in force and their far end absent** — exactly the dangling traversal. Because 0 of the 24 time questions can trigger expansion today, this is a **latent correctness bug, not a current scoring loss**. It becomes a real bug the moment the trigger widens, which is precisely what `siblings()` and `scope_units()` do.

## The literature has never isolated the claim you are testing

The specific claim — that GraphRAG's advantage is relating documents whose links were never called out — is **unevidenced as of October 2026**. No study anywhere holds a corpus fixed, partitions its edges into explicit-recoverable and LLM-only-inferable, and measures the retrieval contribution of the second alone ([multihop_external_evidence.md](/Users/briancorbin/Code/rags/rags-spring-ai/research_notes/Multi%20hop%20questions%20and%20graph%20retrieval/multihop_external_evidence.md)). Microsoft's own paper evaluates community-summary variants judged pairwise on comprehensiveness, diversity, empowerment and directness ([Edge et al., arXiv:2404.16130](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16130v2)) — an **answer-quality** judgment, with no retrieval metric and no latent-link condition.

Worse for the claim, the available evidence points against it. Where edge provenance *has* been ablated, structure beats semantics: removing hierarchical edges collapses performance while dropping sequential or semantic edges costs modestly ([RippleMem, arXiv:2608.13334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13334)). A validation gate removing roughly **103 noisy semantic candidate edges per topic** raised mean recall only from **0.164 to 0.182**, with the authors reading four topics descriptively rather than as significant ([SurveyGraph, arXiv:2610.31317](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31317v1)) — **snippet-level evidence**. The strongest pro-latent data point is LEDGER's `DEPENDS` edges at about **+4.7 points** over a hierarchy-only baseline, with the authors writing that these edges "encode implicit prerequisites that semantic similarity cannot reliably recover" ([LEDGER, arXiv:2606.28379](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28379)). That figure is **snippet-level from Table 9, Appendix E.3, which the fetch did not reach — verify before quoting**; the metric is task consistency, not retrieval; retrieval is single-hop only; and both arms are LLM-inferred. Read straight, it is an argument for *reading* declared dependencies, which is what `defer-to` and `delta` already are.

On depth, the ceiling is two hops and it only pays when the seed is weak. GRAFT's sweep finds one hop clearly insufficient, two hops raising recall substantially, and **a third hop adding nothing at R@20 and 0.005 at R@50** ([GRAFT, arXiv:2608.22381](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22381v1)) — the depth numbers are **snippet-level from Appendix B.3**. HELP's QA F1 peaks at **N=3, 76.18%**, but the whole sweep stays above **74.5%** while retrieval time reaches **577.2 s** at N=3 and is called prohibitive at N=4 ([HELP, arXiv:2602.20926](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20926v1)) — answer-quality metric. G-Long reports 2-hop expansion degrading performance through hub-node drift and ships L=1 ([G-Long, arXiv:2606.13115](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13115)) — snippet-level. **This softens the earlier note's conclusion that "one hop is where the value is"; the reconciled shape is that two hops is the ceiling and whether hop 1→2 pays depends entirely on seed strength.** The sharpest number: HippoRAG's own ablation shows adding one-hop neighbours **without** a weighting mechanism is *worse than not expanding at all* — MuSiQue R@2 falls 37.1 → **25.4**, HotpotQA 55.9 → **47.8** ([HippoRAG, arXiv:2405.14831](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14831v3)). The graph's whole benefit there lives in the weighting.

The one published study on traversing **author-written** links in technical documentation is a null. LARAG scored **F1 0.8382 against 0.8378 for simply retrieving twice as many chunks** — a +0.0004 difference — at 1.4× the latency, while cutting tokens about 18% (1,527 against 1,853) ([LARAG, arXiv:2605.07517](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07517v1)). It is BERTScore against reference answers, so **answer quality, not retrieval**; there is no reranker in the baseline or the pipeline; and it is 20 expert queries on one corpus. Its tuning sweep's best F1 was **(1,3,1) at 0.8276**, yet they shipped **(1,1,1)**, with the deeper and broader settings retrieving about 40 chunks for about 0.80 — same depth, 5× the fan-out, 2.8 F1 points worse. **Fan-out, not depth, is the controlling variable.** And the authors state explicitly that they did not combine authored links with induced semantic edges, which is the exact experiment this decision needs and which nobody has run.

The authored-structure results that *do* show large gains are not prose links. GraphSkill traverses a documentation hierarchy and reports retrieval F1 rising from **about 28% to 79%** ([GraphSkill, arXiv:2603.06620](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06620)), against a flat agentic baseline on the authors' own benchmark. Code-Craft uses Language Server Protocol references — compiler-resolved, not inferred — for up to **82% relative improvement in top-1 retrieval precision** ([Code-Craft, arXiv:2504.08975](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08975v1)). Our typed relations are closer to that class than to a bare hyperlink. That is the one honest reason for optimism, and it argues for a **typed, bounded expansion**, not a graph index.

Three more facts close the case. Inferring links in a corpus like ours is a solved-and-poor problem: requirements traceability reports recovered-link precision of roughly **15–34%** at usable cutoffs — TraceRank's baselines at LinkRecall@30 ~50–64% but ReviewPrecision@30 only ~15–19% ([TraceRank, Frontiers in Computer Science 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1960635/full), snippet-level), and a 2026 multimodal approach at **precision .338, recall .287, F1 .310** ([arXiv:2610.34503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34503)). An authored `validates` edge is 100% precise and free. Next, small corpora are the wrong size for a graph: the only scaling experiment finds plain RAG **outperforming** HippoRAG 2 at the smallest corpus size on both fact retrieval and complex reasoning ([arXiv:2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05690)) — **but the ~56k-token figures (58.64% → 43.20%) are unverified search-snippet extraction of Appendix G.5, and 58.64 also appears as a different cell in the main table, so the corpus attribution may be wrong; verify before publishing.** What *is* verified by direct fetch is the density: on the Medical corpus, LLM extraction produced average degree **1.05**, average connected component size **3.15**, and **0.48 non-isolated entities** ([arXiv:2506.05690v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690v2)). More than half the entities are isolated. A graph you cannot traverse three nodes into cannot deliver multi-hop traversal whatever the depth parameter says. Finally, the benchmarks that produce the biggest graph wins are the least able to prove them: a single-hop BERT reader reaches **67 F1 on HotpotQA** and humans answer **over 80%** of questions shown incomplete evidence ([Min et al., arXiv:1906.02900](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02900)), Chen and Durrett find **more than half** of HotpotQA and WikiHop do not require multi-hop reasoning ([NAACL 2019, N19-1405](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1405/)), and graph margins run inverse to benchmark validity — largest on 2WikiMultihopQA, which is template-generated from Wikidata relation paths. The circulating precise percentages (46%, 27%, 35/26/8/27) are all **secondary and unverified**.

One structural caveat covers all of it: **across the whole surveyed literature there are zero papers evaluating graph or link traversal against a tuned BM25+dense+cross-encoder baseline.** Ours is BM25+dense+RRF+cross-encoder — the strongest seed in this literature — and every paper that strengthened its baseline saw the expansion gain shrink. Estimating the residual gain at our baseline strength is extrapolation past the end of the data.

## What to do instead, cheapest first

**First, recover the ~2,000 declared relation values the parser is dropping.** `target` at ~45% loss, `defer-to` at ~75%, plus `grounding` (469), `specid` (22), `spec-risk status`/`by` (360/242) and `spec-triage layer` (~168), none extracted. These are exact, free, already written down, and this is the highest-value repair per line of code in the whole analysis. The declared-side counts are taken as given from `declared_meta.md` and were not re-derived from the spectastic source HTML.

**Second, resolve page-ended edges to units.** 1,218 u→p rows, including **100% of `typed:defer-to`** — parsed, stored, then discarded by `typed_neighbours`'s `kind == "u"` test. `_best_in_page` already does this for `typed_pages`. Making `node_of` fall back to a page's first unit rather than the page would convert a large share of the 1,199 p→u `href:same-page` rows too. This is not cosmetic: `defer-to` is the mechanism behind **four of the eleven chains** (G-031, G-032, H-003, H-004), and H-012's unfollowable hyperlink is a sample from this class, not a one-off. Unmeasured: how often `_best_in_page` picks the right unit, which needs a run.

**Third, treat the trigger as the subject, not the graph.** 3.8% of questions can have anything inserted today. `siblings()` and `scope_units()` already widen it with no graph at all, and `sibling-spec` reaches **8/8** held-out misses as a reachability ceiling. But the warning is measured, not modelled: the one broad expansion actually run, EpiB, scored **36/47** and displaced nine hits, four of them `identifier`-stratum, because 240 candidates per query cannot enter a top-4 budget without a scoring stage strong enough to reorder them — and the stage it used was RRF by link weight, not the cross-encoder. The literature agrees from the other side: bounded slots and weighted propagation are the only patterns with a positive record. **No measurement exists of a sibling edge scored by the cross-encoder. That is the single most valuable experiment the saved artefacts cannot supply.**

**Fourth, give edges validity intervals before widening anything.** The intersection of the two endpoints' intervals is deterministic and needs no re-parse of history — it is exactly the computation behind the 27.6%-at-position-300 table. It is only a lower bound: an edge can also be added or removed while both endpoints exist, which needs per-revision parsing to detect. Do this before the trigger widens, because a sibling-directory relation is itself time-sensitive — a page that did not exist at the cutoff is still a sibling by path.

**Fifth, if the series wants a time story, build it on `#changelog` → proposal.** It is a real `<a href>`, 119 of 156 date references are already declared, and it is the only edge that explains *why* a version changed. The metric is currently blind to it: G-067–G-071's answers name proposal slugs that no `expected` address contains. Scoring it is a golden-set change and therefore yours.

**Sixth, note that `principles.html` is not in the corpus at all.** 1,675 bare `P-N` mentions against 202 hyperlinks, pointing at a document the 627 pages do not include. A missing document is not a graph problem and no extraction fixes it.

**Do not build** same-anchor-id edges (86 pairs, 0 correct, plus 573 vacuous — and 0/8 measured reach), cross-spec concept co-reference inference, or any depth beyond one. And if a hop stage is ever trialled, the defensible objective from LARAG is **context efficiency at equal answer quality**, not a quality win. The cheaper experiment, if you want one for the series, is the query-side arm: a decomposition stage over the existing pipeline is a prompt, not an index — though note that decomposition alone *loses* on two of three benchmarks, at −4.0 EM against flat RAG on MuSiQue and −2.8 on HotpotQA ([GRASP, arXiv:2605.16598](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16598)).

## Defects the notes turned up in our own artefacts

These are actionable whatever you decide about the graph. The `timecorpus.py` docstring says **14,225 unit-states**; the saved artefact has **15,616**, so the ratio against 10,540 unit keys is **1.48x, not the quoted 1.3x**. **1,025 of the 12,283 edge rows are exact duplicates**, harmless because `typed_neighbours` de-duplicates on the way out, but raw row counts overstate the relation count by about 8%. **All 166 `typed:defer-to` edges are parsed, stored and then dropped**, and the parser additionally loses ~45% of declared `target` and ~75% of declared `defer-to`. And **H-010 and R-004 have no diagnosis in any saved `.md`**, with their expected addresses absent from the top 50.

## Conclusion

The cleanest way to state this for the series is that spectastic is a **schema-governed** corpus, and that is a different animal from the corpora GraphRAG was built for. Its relations are either declared in attributes and hyperlinks, or implied by a directory grammar that the archive's own requirements and TypeScript validators already state in machine-readable form. An LLM extractor would be rediscovering a grammar written down in `packages/schema/src/rules/*.ts`, at the price of the ~30.5% node redundancy, the missing entity resolution, and the open bug that silently discards distinct entities sharing a title. The corpus does not have a latent-link problem. It has a **parser-loss problem, a page-granularity problem and a trigger problem** — and all three are cheap, exact and measurable.

The episode-shaped conclusion is sharper than the engineering one. A graph arm **cannot be ratcheted on the main set**: the main set offers it zero Hit@4 and four all-evidence@4, three of which are reranking problems a cheaper arm also fixes. Any honest case has to be argued on the held-out set, which the episode spends once — so it would have to be made in advance, from the shape of the failures rather than from a measured win. And the failure shape that survives every subtraction is very specific: **a second expected address, in a different document from the one the question names, linked only by prose, that no saved configuration has ever retrieved within 50.** That is two questions in its purest form (H-006, H-007 hop 2) and five with the same-document variant included. Anchored typed expansion is already shipped and already shown not to reach them. The interesting blog post is not "we built a graph". It is "we counted the hops, found eleven of them one hop deep and no bridges at all, found seven in ten of our relations already written down, and spent the episode reading the links we had instead of inventing the ones we did not".
